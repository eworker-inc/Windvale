Latest story 10 August 2026 · Day 13

Windvale is becoming its own workshop.

The current Windows-native path is reconstructing the compiler and its surrounding toolchain: lowerer, packagers, publisher, inspector, and linker. These remain candidate results while independent Linux proof and promotion are pending. AI usage currently runs about $10–$15 per day, heavily subsidized for now.

  3. Windvale holds a newly reconstructed component beside an unfinished cross-host test bench. The caption reads, “Not finished. Not yet promoted. But increasingly Windvale-built.”
  4. An AI meter reports $10–$15 per day beside a power cable tagged “Heavily subsidized—for now.” Windvale says, “Even the price is still experimental.” The closing line reads, “Day 13: Windvale is becoming its own workshop.”
Earlier story 8 August 2026 · Day 11

The messy middle is moving.

Windvale is moving more of its compiler and tools into Windvale itself. A native compiler seed now rebuilds the accepted compiler to exact bytes without .NET on the working Windows route, while Linux execution and broader qualification remain. The workshop is cluttered, but the transfer is real.

  1. “Windvale had started moving out of .NET.” Windvale packs plans beside a new native work area. C# says, “Take your time.”
  2. “In the middle, everything was everywhere.” Compiler, runtime, linker, and test boxes sit among cables and temporary scaffolding. Windvale says, “I know where most of it goes.”
  3. “Then the native compiler seed arrived.” Windvale carries a glowing compiler seed beside prepared Windows and Linux crates. C# holds the recovery toolbox and asks, “Same compiler?” Windvale answers, “Exact bytes.”
  4. A monitor in the working native area reports “Rebuilt.” C# asks, “Done?” Windvale answers, “No. But this is real progress.” The closing line reads, “Day 11: The messy middle is moving.”
Earlier story 6 August 2026 · Day 9

Windvale outgrew its browser bootstrap.

The slow browser path turned out to be interpreting the compiler. Windvale compiled that compiler to direct WebAssembly, cut the measured playground run to 2.1 seconds, and let C# retire from the normal browser path while keeping the recovery kit.

  2. “So Windvale compiled the compiler.” Windvale holds up a glowing block labelled WASM. C# asks, “You can do that?”
  3. “No Blazor. No .NET in the browser.” The playground reports “Main returned 42” in 2.1 seconds.
  4. C# closes a toolbox labelled Recovery and says, “Call if you need me.” Windvale hugs C# and answers, “I will.” The closing line reads, “Day 9: Windvale outgrew its browser bootstrap.”

Architecture portrait August 2026

Confusing ourselves into a computing stack.

An early project portrait embraces the uncertainty: language, compiler layers, runtime, effects, concurrency, platforms, observability, and a great many arrows—all progressing beautifully.

Read the illustrated transcript

The headline reads, “To confuse your competition, you must first confuse yourself.” A crowded Windvale whiteboard connects source, frontend, middle-end, backend, type system, IR layers, effects, memory model, concurrency, runtime, observability, platforms, and distributed systems.

Onlookers ask, “What is that thing?”, “Is it a language?”, and “I don't even know anymore.” The builders answer, “I have a feeling this is revolutionary. (Or insane.)” and “I understand every word, and nothing at the same time.” The closing line says Windvale is progressing beautifully while still figuring out exactly what it has created.
